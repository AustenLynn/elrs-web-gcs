Bench results and frozen goals

Five armed bench runs flown from the same laptop: local Wi-Fi without video, the Cloudflare tunnel without video, two local runs with the FPV video playing on a laptop and a phone at the same time, and the same video run after moving the Pi's hotspot from 2.4 GHz (shared with the ELRS radio) to 5 GHz. On 2026-11-07 the video latency (C2) was measured glass to glass, locally and through the tunnel. Every number comes from the per-command logs of the browser, the gateway and crsf-core, the viewers' WebRTC statistics and the C2 screenshots.

The frozen goals

Approved on 2026-11-07 and not to be changed. Every final run uses the same conditions: props off, hotspot IoT-05 on 5 GHz (channel 36), video playing on two viewers (laptop and phone), each control run at least 5 minutes armed. A percentage passes only if its Wilson 95 % one-sided lower bound clears the target, so the sample size counts, not just the raw rate.

"Bench result" is the run in the goal's own condition. "Margin" is the room for failure: how far that result sits from the limit. "Worst bench run" is the worst run of any condition, to show what happens outside the agreed conditions.

Replay of 500 real commands

500 consecutive commands from the middle of each run, replayed in slow motion. Each dot is one command; it moves through each segment for as long as that command really took there. Hollow dots never reached the radio: they were replaced by a newer command in the same 4 ms frame, or dropped as stale.

The five runs side by side

Total = network + gateway + core, from the browser's send to the full 26-byte serial write. "Within 50 ms" counts every command the browser sent, and adds the clock-sync uncertainty (RTT/2) before comparing. Through the tunnel RTT/2 is about 30 ms, so no command can be shown under 50 ms with that margin, even though the estimate puts 91 % there. Process capability against the 50 ms limit

Only an upper limit exists (latency has no lower limit), so the indices are one-sided: Ppk = Ppu. Common targets: 1.33 is capable, 1.67 is good. Calculated on the commands that reached the radio; the commands that never did are in the attribute row of the first table.

Which index to quote

The data fails the normality test badly (A² in the thousands) and has a long right tail from Wi-Fi bursts. The normal Ppk assumes the tail away and predicts almost no command over 50 ms, while 696 per million actually were (local). Quote the percentile (ISO) Ppk, which uses the data's own 99.865th percentile, and show the observed PPM next to it.

Video latency, glass to glass (C2)

A millisecond clock runs on the laptop screen and the drone camera films it. The same screen shows the video page, so every screenshot holds two times: the clock now, and the clock as it looked when the camera saw it. The difference is everything between the camera and the viewer's screen: air unit, goggles, HDMI capture, H.264 encoding on the Pi, WebRTC and the browser.

Evidence behind the goals

Control latency with three denominators

All figures include the clock-sync uncertainty (RTT/2). "All sent" counts commands sent before arming and after disarming. How to read these results in the report

Video costs about 1 ms at the median, but widens the tail

With two viewers playing, the median rises only from 4.6 to 5.7–5.8 ms, but p99 rises from 18.6 to 26–28 ms and the commands over 50 ms rise from 696 to 1,777–5,037 per million. The software still adds about 2 ms (gateway 0.05 ms, core 2 ms); the rest and all the spread is Wi-Fi.

Run 2 fails on the denominator, not on speed

99.47 % of the commands that reached the radio did so within 50 ms. What fails is a 306 ms Wi-Fi stall at 21:44:09 that tripped the 300 ms command timeout (landing, then failsafe) and 6.9 % of armed commands replaced in the same 4 ms frame because Wi-Fi delivered them in bursts.

Moving the hotspot to 5 GHz removed the video penalty

On 2.4 GHz channel 1 the hotspot shared the band with the 1 W ELRS transmitter. On 5 GHz, with the same two viewers playing, the median (4.64 ms) is no different from the no-video run (Mann-Whitney p = 0.34), p99 drops from 28 to 11 ms, replaced commands from 6.9 % to 0.1 % of armed commands, and both viewers hold 30 fps with no dropped frames.

What 5 GHz did not fix

Two command timeouts (22:29:41 and 22:31:04) still sent the drone to landing. In the first the laptop's browser itself paused 143 ms and the network added about 270 ms more; in the second commands arrived steadily but about 220 ms old, the pattern of a WebSocket (TCP) queue held behind a lost Wi-Fi frame. These are rare events (2 in 5 minutes), not a slow link.

The tunnel costs about 29 ms

The median rises from 4.5 to 33.2 ms, almost all of it in the network segment. The tunnel run is a comparison, never a qualifying run: the criterion requires local routing, and its clock uncertainty (about ±30 ms) is too large to prove any command under 50 ms.

Video lags about 0.7 s, and the old 300 ms idea was out of reach

Locally the video arrives 704 ms after the event (mean, 23 screenshots, max 808 ms); with the page through the tunnel, 865 ms (max 959 ms). Only about 30 ms of that is the viewer's jitter buffer; the rest is the capture, encoding and goggle chain. The goal was set at 1 s (1.2 s via tunnel) from these numbers. The video only shows what the drone sees; commands do not wait for it.

The tunnel video goal has a condition

There is no TURN relay, so WebRTC media goes straight to the Pi's addresses even when the page comes through the tunnel. The tunnel C2 was measured from a viewer on the same Wi-Fi; a viewer on mobile data may get no video at all. That is written into goal G3t.

Where it stops
